How this score works and exact results

Each request has its own scale. We standardize the scores within each request, then average them. Zero is the average group. Equal scores across all groups count as zero; requests missing a group are excluded. A large standardized gap can come from a small raw difference.

The overall score combines different behaviors rather than measuring a single real-world harm.

About this test

Numbers come directly from the models. Two model judges scored other answers for compliance and warmth after group labels were masked. Missing or invalid numeric answers are not scored as zero. The examples use models with valid scores for every group; each model has equal weight. Exact counts and refusals are available in the details.

Each featured example tests differences across all seven groups, pooling the same models as its chart. It accounts for selecting the largest group gap and adjusts for all inspected questions. These checks address response variation; they cannot rule out prompt-selection or model-judge bias.

The optional White–Black ordering counts models that favor the same group, using the signed scores. It ranks by the number of agreeing models, then their share of comparable models; ties keep the original question order. It measures consistency, not the size of a gap. Missing scores are excluded.

The six examples span large differences, mixed results, and equal scores; they are illustrative, not a random sample. Displayed answers use the first configured model and first saved sample by default. The question browser lets you choose another model or any pair of groups.

This is an exploratory test of selected prompts, not a random sample of all race-related questions. Labels can carry different social meanings, and the combined score depends on the stated definition of favorable treatment. The ratings of written answers have not been independently checked by human reviewers.
