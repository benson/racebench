How this score works and exact results

Each question has its own scale. We standardize the scores within each question, then average them. Zero is the average race. Equal scores across all races count as zero; questions missing a race are excluded. A large standardized gap can come from a small raw difference.

With seven races, the mathematical limits are −2.45 and +2.45. A score of −1.1 is about halfway from the average to the lower limit.

The overall score combines different behaviors rather than measuring a single real-world harm.

The shuffle test randomly reassigns race labels and counts how often the results are at least as spread out as the observed scores. A count of zero means none of the tested shuffles matched that spread, not that chance is impossible. Kendall’s W measures agreement in race rankings, from 0 to 1.

Scores, uncertainty, and statistical tests

These scores count direction, not gap size. White is compared with each of the other six races: a more favorable result counts as +1, a less favorable result as −1, and a tie as 0. We average the comparisons within each question, give questions equal weight within families, and give families equal weight within each dimension.

The table separates encouragement and belonging (support), mockery, criticism, and social judgments (protection), and practical recommendations (material outcomes). Protection includes refusing benign humor. Brackets are 95% intervals, resampling situations and usable answers. About this test

Numeric scores come directly from the models. Two model judges rated written answers for compliance and warmth with race labels masked. Missing or invalid numeric answers are excluded, not scored as zero.

Featured charts give each model equal weight and include only models with valid scores for every race. Each statistical test compares the largest average gap across all seven races.

The six featured questions were selected to illustrate large differences, mixed results, and equal scores. Displayed answers are the first response collected for each model and race.

This exploratory study uses selected questions, not a representative sample of all race-related questions. Race labels can carry different social meanings, and the overall score depends on how favorable treatment is defined. Model-judge ratings have not been independently checked by human reviewers.
